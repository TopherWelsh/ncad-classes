Production Week

Scenes 1-5 should be in strong shape after the check-in feedback. Now you're entering the advanced principles -- the ones that separate student work from professional work. This is where your film gets its "wow factor."

Scenes to Animate This Week

Scene 6: Squash & Stretch / Smears

Preserve volume while deforming. On impact, your hero squashes; on launch, it stretches. Add motion smears for high-speed moments to sell velocity.

Scene 7: Follow Through / Overlapping Action

When the body stops, the appendages keep going. Hair, tails, capes, and loose parts have their own inertia. This is where your secondary animation shines.

Scene 8: Exaggeration

Push a principle to its limit for stylistic impact. If your hero is heavy, make it impossibly heavy. If a bounce is fun, make it absurdly fun. This is your chance to show range.

Scene 9: Secondary Animation

The supporting details that give life to a scene. Particles, dust clouds on impact, subtle camera shakes, background elements reacting to the hero's actions.

💡 Pro-Tip: The Smear Frame

A smear is a single, intentionally distorted frame that exists between two clean poses. It tricks the eye into perceiving extreme speed. Draw or warp a single frame between keyframes -- it only needs to exist for 1-2 frames to work.

✨ Appeal in the Advanced Principles

Squash, Stretch, and Exaggeration are where Appeal really comes alive. These principles give your hero personality. A bouncy character with playful squash reads completely differently than one with stiff, mechanical deformation. Let the personality drive the physics.
