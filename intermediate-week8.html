VFX Shot Planning + 3D Camera Tracking

Plan, shoot, track, and composite digital objects into real-world video. This week is part field trip, part VFX lab, and part production workflow.

Discord Student Resources Drive Week 9 Production

What We Are Building

You will create three short VFX shots where artwork, text, 3D layers, or 3D models feel like they belong inside real-world footage. Each shot should be 5-10 seconds long and can be portrait or landscape.

🎥 Today's Two-Part Lesson

Part 1: how to plan and shoot footage that can actually be tracked. Part 2: how to bring that footage into After Effects, use the 3D Camera Tracker, create a matching camera/null, and composite digital objects into the shot.

The Assignment

3 Finished VFX Shots

  • Quantity: 3 separate shots.
  • Length: 5-10 seconds each.
  • Format: Portrait or landscape.
  • Goal: Composite a digital element into real footage so it feels grounded in the scene.

What Can Be Composited?

  • 3D text attached to a wall, floor, sign, desk, or sidewalk.
  • 2D artwork placed into a real scene as signage, stickers, posters, screens, or objects.
  • 3D model/object integrated into footage with lighting, shadow, and color matching.
  • Motion graphic elements that feel physically connected to camera movement.

What Makes It Work?

  • Clean camera track.
  • Stable placement in 3D space.
  • Correct scale and perspective.
  • Matching light, color, grain, blur, and shadows.

Before We Shoot: Think Like a VFX Artist

Shot Planning Checklist

  • What is the effect? Know what you are adding before you shoot.
  • Where does it live? Floor, wall, table, sign, sidewalk, doorway, sky, screen, or object.
  • What plane are you tracking? Find a flat surface with texture and visible detail.
  • What blocks it? Plan foreground objects if you want the composite to pass behind something.
  • What is the camera doing? Push in, pan, tilt, walk-by, orbit, or handheld move.
  • Is there enough detail? Blank walls and shiny glass are hard to track.
  • Is the lighting readable? Notice shadow direction, color temperature, and brightness.
  • Is the shot long enough? Hold the move long enough to get usable seconds.
  • Is it stable enough? Handheld is fine, but chaotic shaking creates bad tracks.
  • Did you shoot options? Get extra takes. Bad footage wastes more time than extra footage.

Field Trip Shooting Rules

Phone / iPhone Shooting Basics

  • Clean the lens: Seriously. Do this first.
  • Use 4K if available: More detail gives the tracker more information.
  • Use 24 or 30 fps: Keep the frame rate normal and consistent.
  • Lock exposure/focus: Avoid brightness pulsing while you move.
  • Avoid digital zoom: Move your body instead.
  • Hold before and after: Give each shot a few clean seconds at the start and end.

Camera Moves That Track Well

  • Slow push-in: Move toward the tracked plane.
  • Sideways slide: Move parallel to a wall, desk, sidewalk, or sign.
  • Small orbit: Move slightly around an object or corner.
  • Tilt/reveal: Reveal a surface with visible detail.
  • Avoid: fast whip pans, blurry footage, blank walls, reflections, and people crossing the whole frame.
⚠️ The Tracker Cannot Save Bad Footage

If the footage has no texture, too much motion blur, extreme shakiness, or giant moving objects covering the frame, After Effects will struggle. Shoot for the tracker first. Make the footage interesting second.

Good Shot Ideas

Easy

  • 3D text sitting on a table or sidewalk.
  • Animated poster placed on a wall.
  • Logo or graphic attached to a sign.
  • Glowing portal/card/object on a flat surface.

Medium

  • 3D object on a desk with fake shadow.
  • Animated graffiti tracked onto a wall.
  • Floating UI panel locked to a real surface.
  • Object passes behind a foreground pole/person/door frame using masks.

Ambitious

  • 3D model integrated with matching light and shadow.
  • Camera move around an inserted object.
  • Multiple objects on different planes.
  • Scene extension or surreal environmental addition.

After Effects 3D Camera Tracker Workflow

1. Import Bring in footage and make a comp from the clip.
2. Track Select footage, then Animation > Track Camera.
3. Solve Wait for AE to analyze and solve the camera.
4. Place Select good points, create Camera + Null/Solid/Text.
5. Composite Match color, blur, grain, light, shadow, and occlusion.

What to Click in AE

  • Select your footage layer.
  • Go to Animation > Track Camera.
  • Wait for Analyzing and Solving Camera.
  • Hover over track points until the red target sits on the correct plane.
  • Right-click and choose Create Null and Camera, Create Text and Camera, or Create Solid and Camera.
  • Parent your graphic/object to the tracked null or replace the created solid.

Composite Polish Pass

  • Scale: Does the object feel the correct size?
  • Perspective: Does it sit on the same plane?
  • Lighting: Does it match the direction and softness of the real shot?
  • Color: Does it match the footage temperature and contrast?
  • Blur/grain: Does it look too sharp or too clean?
  • Occlusion: Should anything pass in front of it?

Common Problems

Analysis Solve Failed

The footage may be too blurry, too blank, too shaky, or full of moving subjects. Try Detailed Analysis, trim to the cleanest part, or choose a better shot.

Object Slides Around

Your points may not be on the same plane, or the track is weak. Choose a better cluster of points and set the object on a stable surface.

Object Looks Fake

It may be too sharp, too bright, the wrong color, missing shadow, or not masked behind foreground objects. Composite polish matters.
