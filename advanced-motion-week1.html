Animated Infographics

Turn information into visual storytelling. Use motion to explain relationships, direct attention, and make an idea easier to understand.

Discord Google Drive Storyboard Template

The Big Idea

An animated infographic is a visual argument. You decide what matters, what relationship the audience needs to see, what visual language represents it, and when each idea is introduced.

What You Should Understand Today

See the relationship

Identify whether the audience must understand comparison, cause and effect, change, accumulation, hierarchy, flow, sequence, correlation, or feedback.

Build a visual grammar

Give color, shape, size, position, typography, and motion consistent meanings the viewer can learn quickly.

Direct attention over time

Use framing, timing, contrast, isolation, transitions, rhythm, and sound to create one clear focal point at a time.

Design honestly

Check sources, scales, comparisons, credits, and context so the animation clarifies information instead of distorting it.

The Four-Stage Translation

Do not jump directly from numbers or facts to animation. Find the relationship and meaning first.

1. Data

Numbers, dates, quantities, categories, facts, and observations.

2. Information

Patterns, comparisons, relationships, sequences, and trends.

3. Meaning

Growing, shrinking, unusual, dangerous, connected, unequal, or changing.

4. Experience

Clarity, surprise, urgency, curiosity, empathy, or action.

Build the Visual Language

Start with a verb

Compare. Accumulate. Transform. Connect. Rank. Flow. Cause. Sequence. Repeat. Animate the relationship—not merely the objects.

Create a motif

Use one recurring visual idea or behavior to organize the piece. A motif should carry meaning, not merely decorate.

Use abstraction

Photograph → illustration → silhouette → icon → dot. Choose the simplest form that preserves the meaning.

Choose the right model

Bars compare. Lines show change. Maps show location. Timelines show sequence. Networks show connections. Choose by purpose.

Choreograph information

Stage the subject, main graphic, important number, and annotation. Give the viewer time to land, read, understand, and reset.

Make transitions explain

A transition should reveal a relationship or preserve continuity. Ask why one object becomes another.

⚠️ The PowerPoint Test

If the piece is still equally effective as six centered slides with titles, icons, and generic fades, the motion is not doing enough storytelling work. Build one visual world, turn nouns into actions, connect ideas through transitions, and vary the rhythm.

Story Structure

Hook → Setup → Explanation → Payoff

Earn attention, establish the question, reveal the relationship, and arrive at a clear insight. The piece should answer WHAT?, SO WHAT?, and NOW WHAT?

Audio + Visuals

Let narration provide the claim while graphics provide evidence. Use sound to reinforce timing, precision, growth, impact, volume, or emotional tone—not simply to fill silence.

Project 1 Assignment

Explain One Thing in 15–30 Seconds

Create a polished animated infographic explaining a simple, funny, useful, surprising, or otherwise engaging concept. You may combine 2D, 3D, typography, character, data, diagrams, collage, talking-head footage, live action, or other techniques—as long as every choice supports the message.

Your piece must

  • Communicate one focused concept to a defined audience.
  • Establish a clear hook, progression, and payoff.
  • Use a deliberate motif or visual rule set.
  • Lead the viewer's eye through every shot.
  • Use visuals to explain—not simply repeat text or narration.
  • Maintain readable pacing and a consistent visual language.
  • Credit factual information and third-party assets.
  • Reach demo-reel quality.

Technical starting points

  • Duration: 15–30 seconds.
  • Landscape: 1920 × 1080.
  • Square: 1080 × 1080.
  • Portrait: 1080 × 1920.
  • Frame rate: Choose intentionally and keep it consistent.
  • Style: Any coherent technique mix.

Self-Critique Before You Leave

What relationship am I revealing?

Name the verb—not just the topic.

What does the movement mean?

Every major motion choice should have a job.

Where does the eye look first?

Make hierarchy visible in every frame.

What do the graphics explain?

They should add understanding beyond the words.

Is there enough time?

Readability matters more than packing in another beat.

Is there a payoff?

End with a clear insight, result, or change.
