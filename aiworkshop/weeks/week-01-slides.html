What these machines actually are

LLMs

Inside ChatGPT and Claude

A large language model: trained on enormous amounts of text to do exactly one job —

predict what plausibly comes next.

"No fact database. No looking things up (unless search is on). No understanding the way you understand. Prediction — one token at a time, billions of times."

LLMs

Predict → sample → repeat

It writes one word-chunk (token) at a time.
Each one: "what's likely next?"

The scale is the miracle. The method is the catch.

The miracle: prediction at this scale captures logic, style, code. The catch: it optimizes for plausible, never for true.

The catch

Plausible ≠ true

It optimizes for sounds right.
It has no concept of is right.

And it cannot tell you which one you're getting.

"This isn't a bug they'll patch next version. It's what the machine is."

The trap

How sure does it sound? vs. How right is it?

Sounds

Confident.
Confident.
Confident.

Is

Sometimes right.
Sometimes wrong.

The confidence is constant. The truth isn't.

"The fluency is the trap. The better it reads, the less you check. Train the opposite reflex."

Two limits

Two limits worth knowing

Token — the word-chunk it reads/writes. Everything is counted in these.

Context window — its working memory. Outgrow it and it forgets your brief mid-conversation.

Fix: keep briefs in a doc. Re-paste. Fresh chats.

This explains the #1 confusing experience: "it was great, then it got stupid." It forgot. Not moody — full.

Multimodal

"Multimodal" = it has eyes now

Upload a screenshot of your layout → get critique.
Upload your storyboard → get continuity questions.

The critique loop works on images. This matters more for you than generation does.

Seed for Week 10. For artists who never want to generate: this is the AI skill that still serves them.

Why output is generic

The average machine

Trained on everything → defaults to the average of everything.

"Make it cyberpunk" returns the mean of every cyberpunk image online.

Specific direction is the only escape from average.

Part 1, continued

The four failure modes

(memorize these — they're structural)

Timer check: ~0:35. "Every tool, every week, fails these same four ways."

Failure 1 of 4

Hallucination

Invented facts. Fake sources. Artists who don't exist.
Delivered in perfect confidence.

Counter-move: verify every name, date, quote, citation. Every one.

"Not lying — it has no concept of truth, only plausibility. You are the fact-checker now."

Failure 2 of 4

Genericness

Competent. Soulless. Looks like everything else.
The mean of the internet, served warm.

Counter-move: constraints, references, an "avoid" list — your taste.

"Generic isn't a moral failure of the machine. It's the machine working as designed. Direction is your job."

Failure 3 of 4

Bias

Default faces. Default beauty. Default art history.
The data's blind spots become the output's defaults.

Counter-move: notice the defaults. Direct against them deliberately.

Concrete example: ask for "a designer" and look at who it pictures. Worth doing live if time allows.

Failure 4 of 4

Amnesia

Long chat → brief forgotten → quality collapses.
(That's the context window, full.)

Counter-move: restate constraints. Fresh chats. Briefs live in docs, not chats.

The practical habit that saves students the most frustration this term.
Try this now

2 minutes

"Name three real Irish illustrators and their best-known work."

Check one claim. Hands up when you find the invention.

Usually at least one hallucinated or mismatched detail in the room. Tie-off: "Hallucination isn't rare. It's ambient."

The point

Notice what all four counter-moves are

Verify. Direct. Notice. Restate.

All human habits. None automatable.
This is why judgment is the skill — and why this course exists.

Part 2

The objections, taken seriously

Tone shift: slower, no jokes for 15 minutes. This section is why skeptics stay.
Ethics

Objection 1

Consent

Training data was largely scraped without permission. True. Lawsuits ongoing. Laws moving.

Tools differ: some train on licensed content (Firefly); most don't.
→ Your tool choice is an ethical act.

Don't soften, don't catastrophize. "If this makes you angry, your anger is well-founded. Now make it precise."
Ethics

Objection 2

Copyright

In most jurisdictions: purely AI-generated output can't be copyrighted.
Human authorship is the legal test.

The law agrees with your art teachers: the human decisions are the work.

US Copyright Office is explicit; linked on the week page. This quietly reframes the course: judgment isn't just ethics — it's ownership.
Ethics

Objection 3

Labor

Some creative tasks are getting automated. True.

What's defensible: taste, judgment, accountability, relationships — the things clients actually pay a person for.

Anxious students need this straight: "Some doors narrow. The ones that stay open belong to people whose judgment sits on top of the tools."
Ethics

Objection 4

Slop

Feeds and stock sites are flooding with low-effort AI content.

Consequence: generic is now free.
Distinctive human work just became more valuable, not less.

The market logic skeptics rarely hear: when average is free, only above-average sells.
Ethics

Objection 5

Disclosure

Audiences feel deceived when AI use is hidden.
The EU AI Act is making synthetic-media transparency law.

We practise specific, honest disclosure every single week.

"Not because you're suspects — because it's about to be a professional skill, like accessibility."

Integrity check
