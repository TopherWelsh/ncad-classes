The week in one line

AI is your editor and interviewer, never your ghostwriter. The moment every applicant has AI write their cover letter is the moment AI-voice becomes a disqualifier. Generic is free now, so generic is worthless — the branding lesson, aimed at your career.

02Key Concepts

1 · The career reality, without the panic

Some production tasks are automating; the roles that remain reward judgment, taste, accountability, and critical AI use — the spine of this course. The CV reframe: don't write "good at AI tools" (everyone does). Write what's rare and true — you can direct AI, verify its output, and know when to refuse.

2 · Case studies are stories, not galleries

A case study is a narrative: client/brief → problem → process (your decisions, iterations, rejected directions) → outcome. Recruiters read to imagine working with you; the path is the juicy part. AI can't write your path — it wasn't there — but it's excellent at structuring one you feed it.

3 · The extraction interview (the core technique)

Don't ask AI to write your case study. Make it interview you, one question at a time, hunting for the constraint that hurt, the decision you'd defend, the thing you cut, what the client actually said, what you'd do differently. Then: "write using ONLY my answers, my phrasing where possible." Its questions, your answers, your authorship.

4 · The Generic Detector

AI-voice has tells — "elevate," "seamless," "passionate about," "stunning," "user-centric," "I'm excited to" — recognizable to every recruiter because every AI writes them. The skill: spot them (in AI's output and your own drafts) and replace each with something only you could have written.

Watch out

AI also inflates — claiming results you didn't measure, a role bigger than you had. Deflate it. A modest true claim beats an impressive unverifiable one; recruiters check, and getting caught inflating ends candidacies.

5 · Disclosure as a differentiator

When AI helped with a project, specific disclosure reads as confidence and process maturity. "Textures generated, then repainted by hand; concept and art direction mine" tells a recruiter you know exactly where your value sits. Vague "AI-assisted" invites the worst assumption. Start from the disclosure templates.

6 · Job-search systems (AI as research + admin engine)

  • Company/role research: what does this studio make, who are their clients (verify against the real site).
  • Role matching: job ad + your skills → where you fit, where the gap is.
  • Outreach: you write the personal hook; AI structures around it; you de-generic it.
  • Interview prep: "act as the hiring manager and ask me the 8 questions they'd actually ask."
  • Freelance admin: lead trackers, follow-ups, scope questions, rate research.

04Demo: Case Study Rewrite — Generic to Yours

I made this, so can you

One real project written two ways — proving AI is useless as a ghostwriter and powerful as an interviewer.

Prepared demo artifact

Instructor backup / finished example: case-study-diff.html. Use it if the live tool run fails, or to reveal the finished structure after critique.

  1. Generate the generic version (knowingly): "write a portfolio case study for this project." Read it flat; the room calls the stock phrases; highlight them live.
  2. The extraction interview: "don't write yet — interview me," hunting for the constraint that hurt, the decision you'd defend, the thing you cut. Answer with specifics.
  3. The honest rewrite: "write 150 words using ONLY my answers, my phrasing, no adjectives I didn't earn — end with a disclosure line."
  4. Side by side: the room names the differences — specifics, stakes, voice, the disclosure line as a trust feature.
The planned failure

Version 2 still smuggles in 1–2 stock phrases. Find them, replace by hand, on screen — the Generic Detector is never AI's job to finish.

05Step-by-Step Workflows

The case study rewrite (any project)

  1. Generate the generic version knowingly — your "before" and stock-phrase target.
  2. Run the extraction interview — answer honestly, with specifics and stakes.
  3. Rewrite from your answers only — your phrasing, no unearned adjectives, a disclosure line if AI touched the project.
  4. Run the Generic Detector — replace every surviving stock phrase by hand.
  5. Deflate — keep only what's true and defensible in an interview.
  6. The 5-metre test — would a skimming recruiter learn something only-you in two sentences?

Build your job-search prompt pack

  1. Save the five system prompts into one doc.
  2. Customize each with your track, strengths, non-negotiables.
  3. Use them as templates per application — reusable for years.
  4. Add moves that work — the pack is a living career tool.

06Prompt Recipes

The extraction interview (the core)I'm writing a portfolio case study for this project: [2-line description]. Don't write anything yet. Interview me, one question at a time, max 6 questions, hunting for: the constraint that hurt, the decision I'd defend in a fight, the thing I cut that I loved, what the client/audience actually said, and what I'd do differently. After my answers, write a 150-word case study using ONLY my material, my phrasing where possible, no adjectives I didn't earn. End with one line disclosing how AI was used in the project (if at all).
The Generic DetectorRead this portfolio/CV text and flag every phrase that could appear in any creative's portfolio — the AI-voice tells (e.g., "passionate about", "seamless", "elevate", "stunning", "user-centric"). For each, ask me a question that would help me replace it with something only I could have written. Don't rewrite it for me.
The deflatorHere is my case study / CV section: [paste]. Challenge every claim about results, my role, and impact. Which are measurable, which are vague, which might be inflated? Rewrite only the overclaims as modest, defensible, true statements I could back up in an interview.
The role matcherHere is a job ad: [paste]. Here are my real skills and projects: [paste/list]. Where do I genuinely fit this role, where's the honest gap, and what 2 things should I learn or show to close it? Don't flatter me — I need the truth before I apply.
The interview rehearsalAct as the hiring manager for this role: [paste ad]. Ask me the 8 questions you'd actually ask a junior [track] candidate, one at a time. After each of my answers, tell me what was strong and what a real interviewer would still be unsure about. Be a tough but fair interviewer.
The outreach structurer (you write the hook)I want to reach out to [studio/person]. My genuine, specific reason for wanting THEM: [you write this — the part AI can't]. Structure a short, warm, non-generic outreach message around my hook. Keep my voice. No "I hope this email finds you well", no "I'm excited to", no flattery. Under 120 words.

07Lab: Generic Detector + Case Study Rewrite (70 min)

Cold start friendly. Bring a real project; no project? Use your strongest coursework.

  1. Generate + detect (15 min): generate the generic case study; run the Generic Detector; highlight every stock phrase. (Seeing AI write your generic version is the wake-up call.)
  2. Extraction interview (25 min): answer with real specifics and stakes; get the rewrite. This is the assessed deliverable — it must read like you.
  3. Deflate + detect again (15 min): challenge every claim; replace surviving stock phrases by hand. ✏️ the lines only you could have written.
  4. Prompt pack (15 min): customize at least three job-search prompts (role matcher + interview rehearsal + outreach structurer recommended).
Zero-AI track (fully equal)

Partner-run extraction interview (genuinely better interview practice), hand-written case study, peer red-pen for the Generic Detector.

08Integrity Check

This week's four
  1. Does your rewritten case study contain a sentence only you could have written? If not, keep extracting.
  2. Did you deflate every claim to what's true and defensible in an interview?
  3. Is your AI disclosure specific (what AI did, what you did) — not a vague "AI-assisted"?
  4. Your prompt pack uses AI for research/structure/admin and keeps the voice and judgment human.
