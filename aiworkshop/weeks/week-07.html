The economics of this week

A model produces a positioning line, palette, and three taglines in forty seconds — all competent, all interchangeable. Competent-and-interchangeable is the definition of brand failure. Clients never bought assets; they bought judgment. AI made assets nearly free, which makes judgment the entire remaining product.

02Key Concepts

1 · Questions before assets

The amateur move: "design me a logo." The professional move: interrogate first — audience, occasion, budget reality, what it must never feel like, who it's secretly competing with. AI is excellent at asking these questions when told to; only you (and your client) can answer them.

In real work

"They asked things nobody else asked" wins pitches. The question list is the first thing a client sees of your process.

2 · Positioning: the sentence everything hangs on

For [narrow audience], [name] is the [category] that [genuine difference]. AI drafts these fluently — and almost all are soup. The de-souper: strike every word a competitor could also claim. What survives is your difference. If nothing survives, the brand doesn't have one yet — which is a strategy discovery, and billable.

3 · Systems, not assets

  • Voice: 4 adjectives, each with a "this not that" pair ("playful, not wacky").
  • Type: 2 pairings with rationale tied to the positioning — reject any rationale that could justify anything. Licence checked (Google Fonts = clear).
  • Palette: named colours + where each is allowed. Usage rules are the system; swatches are decoration.
  • Layout logic: grid habits, spacing personality.

4 · Copy variants without losing the voice

AI is a variant machine — controlled by a tone spec: your adjectives + this-not-thats + banned words. Without it: LinkedIn voice.

Watch out

Model copy loves "elevate," "seamless," "vibrant," "discover." Ban them by name. A good banned-words list is a brand asset.

5 · The stress test: critique loops for systems

Phone-screen size, sticker size, the support-slot poster where your name is 10% of the layout, the cynical Dublin gig-goer — and accessibility contrast. Then revise the single weakest element: a stress test that ends in a redesign was a tantrum, not a test.

The contrast trap

Models estimate contrast and confidently get it wrong. Check the two text pairs in the real calculator (WebAIM) and record the numbers.

6 · Production: where the hours come back

Asset checklists with specs, export QA lists, naming conventions, alt text drafts, style-guide assembly. Pure friction, fully delegable — with specs verified, because platform dimensions drift and models misremember them.

04Demo: Toy Cathedral — Weak Idea to Campaign Kit

I made this, so can you

"A logo and poster for my synth band" becomes a coherent system in four moves — with the human decisions marked.

Prepared demo artifact

Instructor backup / finished example: campaign-kit.html. Use it if the live tool run fails, or to reveal the finished structure after critique.

  1. Interrogation first. The model asks the 6 questions a brand designer would ask; the instructor answers with personality. The answers are the strategy.
  2. System, not assets. Positioning ×5 (one de-souped live, words struck with the room), voice + this-not-thats, type rationale (one rejected: "could justify any font"), palette with usage rules — [GUESS] marks visible.
  3. Stress test. Phone, sticker, support slot, cynic — weakest element revised, one targeted fix.
  4. The contrast moment. The model says the palette pair passes. WebAIM, live. The model loses the argument — and the habit installs itself.

05Step-by-Step Workflow: Micro-Brand System in One Session

  1. Interrogate: the model asks its 6 questions; you answer in writing. These answers are strategy.
  2. Position: 5 draft lines → de-soup → keep the one no competitor could claim.
  3. Voice: 4 adjectives + this-not-thats + 5 banned words.
  4. Type: 2 Google-Fonts pairings, each with rationale citing the positioning.
  5. Palette: 5 colours + usage rules + both text pairs checked in WebAIM (record the ratios).
  6. Stress test: run the attack; revise the single weakest element.
  7. Checklist: assets per deliverable with specs + export QA list.
  8. Assemble the sheet — and design it. The brand sheet for a brand is a brand artifact.

06Prompt Recipes

The interrogationI want "a logo and poster for [project]". Before you make anything: ask me the 6 questions a senior brand designer would ask first. Number them. Don't answer them yourself.
The system builderFrom my answers, write: (1) a one-line positioning — "for [audience], [name] is the [category] that [difference]"; (2) 4 brand voice adjectives, each with a "this not that" pair; (3) two type-pairing directions with rationale tied to the positioning — Google Fonts only; (4) a 5-colour palette with hex codes AND where each colour is allowed; (5) three layout concepts described in words. Mark anything that's a guess about my audience with [GUESS].
The de-souperHere are 5 positioning lines: [paste]. For each, strike every word that could equally describe a competitor. Show me what's left. Which line has the most surviving words?
The tone spec + copy variantsVoice: [4 adjectives + this-not-that pairs]. Banned words: elevate, seamless, vibrant, discover, [yours]. Write 8 headline variants for [deliverable + audience], in this voice, max 7 words each. Then mark which 2 you think are weakest and why — I decide the winners.
The stress testStress-test this brand system: [paste sheet]. How does it fail at phone-screen size? At sticker size? On a support-slot poster where the name is 10% of the layout? What would a cynical [audience member] say? Which palette pairs likely fail accessibility contrast for text? Identify the single weakest element and propose one targeted revision — not a redesign.
The production checklistBuild the asset/export checklist for [one concrete occasion]: every deliverable with dimensions/format/colour space, plus a pre-export QA list (spelling, contrast, bleed, file naming convention: [yours]). Flag any spec you're not certain is current.

07Lab: Brand System Stress Test (70 min)

Cold start friendly. No micro-brand? Standing brief: a late-night toastie hatch beside a Dublin venue, called "Notions."

  1. Interrogate + position (20 min): 6 questions answered in writing → 5 lines → de-soup → keep the survivor. ✏️ your answers.
  2. System draft (20 min): voice + banned words; 2 type pairings with rationale; palette with usage rules — contrast-check both text pairs in WebAIM and record the numbers.
  3. Stress test (15 min): run the attack; revise the single weakest element; note what and why.
  4. Checklist + sheet (15 min): production checklist for one occasion; assemble and design the mini brand sheet.
Zero-AI path (fully equal)

The interrogation and stress-test question lists are printed in this page — run them on paper. Google Fonts and WebAIM involve no AI at all. This week's lab never requires generation.

08Integrity Check

This week's four
  1. Count your ✏️ marks — are most decisions on the sheet yours? Under half: whose brand is this?
  2. Are [GUESS] labels still visible — or did guesses quietly become facts?
  3. Did you verify contrast with the real checker, not the model's estimate?
  4. Client work from this method: "AI used for drafts and variants; strategy, selection, and final design mine."

09Troubleshooting

Positioning lines are all soupDe-soup harder. If nothing survives, the difference isn't real yet — that's strategy information, and it's valuable
Every type rationale sounds plausibleThat's the tell. Reject any rationale that could justify anything; demand it cite the positioning
Copy sounds like LinkedInNo tone spec. Feed voice + banned words; ban "elevate" by name
Model says contrast is fineModels estimate; WebAIM knows. Check and record the ratios
The stress test praises everythingAnti-sycophancy clause (Week 2) + name the attacker: "a cynical gig-goer who's seen 1,000 posters"

Optional extension

  • Flex test: apply your system to a deliverable you didn't plan for (tote bag, ticket stub, loading screen). Did the rules hold?
  • Run the whole workflow on the standing brief in 25 minutes — speed reveals which steps you actually understand.
  • Hard mode: write the rejection memo — 150 words explaining why you rejected the AI's most plausible direction. Defending a rejection is the purest judgment exercise in design.
