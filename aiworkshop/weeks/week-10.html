Captions aren't garnish: they're an accessibility deliverable with legal weight in the EU — and most social video plays silent, so captions are reach. 02Key Concepts

1 · Transcription: the quiet workhorse

Captions, interview notes, edit-by-text, searchable footage. Its reliable failures cluster exactly where audiences notice: proper nouns ("NCAD" → "encad"), Irish names and places, craft terms ("risograph"), homophones.

The professional pattern

Machine transcribes → human corrects → the corrections table is kept (heard → corrected). That table is QA evidence and disclosure note in one document.

2 · Captions are craft (not autocaption sludge)

  • ≤ 42 characters per line, 2 lines max.
  • Break at sense boundaries — never mid-name.
  • Keep the speaker's rhythm — captions preserve voice, they don't formalize it.
  • Sound information when it matters: [door slams], [music swells].
  • Watch once with the sound OFF — the silent-autoplay test.

3 · Temp VO and the voice consent line

Legitimate: scratch narration for animatics, accessibility reads of your own texts, placeholders you'll re-record — and phone-recorded human scratch is often better for timing anyway.

The brightest line in the course

Never clone a real person's voice without documented consent. Not classmates, not celebrities, not "just for the rough." This is hardening into law — and "it was temporary" does not work.

4 · The multimodal critique relay (works on everything)

  1. Screenshot the WIP — frame, layout, model sheet, UI.
  2. Three named reviewers — 3 observations + 1 priority fix each, no softening.
  3. You filter — accept/reject each point with written reasons. At least one critique will be confidently wrong; rejecting it well is the skill.
  4. Peer challenges one accept and one reject.
  5. Triage: blocker / should-fix / polish, with minutes per fix.
In real work

The triage list with time estimates is a producer's artifact — it shows you can convert feedback into a plan. That's hireable.

5 · Sound briefs and the music rights wall

Describing sound is a deliverable skill ("dry clicks, room tone, one swell at the title"). AI drafts sound briefs from your treatment. Generated music stays out of deliverables — rights contested, active litigation. Scratch-only, private.

I made this, so can you

The instructor records 45–60 seconds live — imperfect, with a stumble — and takes it all the way to broadcast-rule captions, then runs the relay on a real WIP.

Prepared demo artifact

Instructor backup / finished example: captions-pipeline.html. Use it if the live tool run fails, or to reveal the finished structure after critique.

  1. The recording: live, with planted traps — "NCAD," an Irish place name, "risograph," a restart.
  2. The error hunt: raw transcript on screen; the room finds the failures as sport, tallied by type.
  3. The cleanup: caption-ready SRT (line lengths, sense breaks, rhythm preserved) + the corrections table — the teaching object.
  4. The relay: a volunteered WIP through three named reviewers; the volunteer filters aloud with reasons; the room challenges; the triage list assembles itself.
What to notice

The machine did volume; the human did names, rhythm, and judgment. One critique point was confidently wrong and got rejected with a reason — the most professional moment of the demo.

05Step-by-Step Workflows

Caption pipeline (any clip, forever)

  1. Transcribe with whatever's nearest (editor built-in, Descript-class, phone).
  2. Correct against the audio: names, terms, homophones. Log every correction.
  3. Format: ≤42 chars/line, 2 lines max, sense-boundary breaks, rhythm preserved.
  4. Export SRT → import to your editor → watch with sound OFF.
  5. Keep the corrections table with the project.

The critique relay (any WIP, any discipline)

  1. Screenshot the work (yours, or with permission).
  2. Three named reviewers fitted to the work — pick personas who'd disagree.
  3. Demand 3 observations + 1 priority fix each, no softening.
  4. Filter with written reasons — accept/reject every point.
  5. Peer challenge: one accept + one reject defended.
  6. Triage: blocker / should-fix / polish + minutes. Your next session, pre-planned.

06Prompt Recipes

The caption cleanupClean this raw transcript into captions. Fix obvious mis-hearings — it's "NCAD", "risograph", [your names/terms]. Break into caption-length lines: max 42 characters per line, max 2 lines per cue, break at sense boundaries, never mid-name. Keep my actual speech rhythm — don't formalize my voice. Output as SRT with placeholder timings. Then give me a corrections table: heard → corrected.
The three-reviewer relayCritique this [frame/layout/sheet/UI] as three different people: [reviewer 1], [reviewer 2], and an accessibility reviewer. Each gives exactly 3 observations and 1 priority fix. Reference what you can actually see. Don't soften, don't compliment.
The edit triageI've accepted these critique points: [paste your accepted list]. Merge them into one prioritized edit checklist: BLOCKER (breaks the piece) / SHOULD-FIX (hurts it) / POLISH (nice). Estimate minutes per fix. Order by impact-per-minute.
The sound briefFrom this treatment/edit description: [paste], draft a sound brief for a sound designer (or future me): overall feel in 2 sentences, moment-by-moment cues tied to shots, what silence is doing, references as QUALITIES (e.g., "close, dry, single-mic intimacy"), and what to avoid. No music generation — this is a brief for humans.
The footage loggerHere is the transcript of my interview footage with rough timestamps: [paste]. Build me a log: topic per segment, the 5 strongest quotable moments (with timestamp + why), repeated themes, and a paper-edit order suggestion for a [60-second] cut. I make the final selects.

07Lab: Screenshot Critique Relay (70 min)

Cold start friendly. Bring any WIP — or use the provided set.

  1. Record + transcribe (15 min): 45–60 seconds of you pitching your current project. Phone voice memo, imperfect on purpose → transcribe.
  2. Caption pass (20 min): cleanup recipe → corrections table (the assessed artifact) → SRT.
  3. The relay (20 min): your WIP → three reviewers → filter every point in writing with reasons → neighbour challenges one accept and one reject.
  4. Triage (15 min): blocker/should-fix/polish + minutes. Assemble the packet: clip + table + filtered critique + edit list.
Zero-AI track (fully equal)

Hand-transcribe 30 seconds (real subtitlers still do), format to BBC rules, then run the relay on paper with two classmates as named reviewers. The hand-transcription teaches caption rhythm better.

08Integrity Check

This week's four
  1. Captions: did a human (you) verify every name and term against the audio? Autocaption sludge with your name on it is still your sludge.
  2. Voice: every voice in your work is yours, consented, or clearly synthetic-and-labelled. No cloning real people. Ever.
  3. Relay on a classmate's work: permission first, always.
  4. Music: nothing generated in deliverables — rights contested. Scratch-only, private.

09Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
Transcription mangles Irish names/placesIt will. The corrections pass exists for this — and feed correct spellings in the prompt next time
Captions read like a legal documentYou let it formalize. "Keep my actual speech rhythm" — restore the "like"s that are voice, not noise
SRT timing driftsPlaceholder timings need an editor pass — nudge cues to the actual speech. Five minutes
All three reviewers agreePersonas too close. Pick reviewers who'd fight: a minimalist and a maximalist
The critique misreads the imageMultimodal models do that. Reject-with-reason — "it claims the type clips the edge; it doesn't" — that's why you filter
