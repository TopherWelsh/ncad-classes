Part 1

Research hygiene

~0:10.

The good news first

Where AI research genuinely shines

Mapping unfamiliar territory · finding the right questions
widening past page one of Pinterest · summarizing the boring PDFs

The biggest honest speed-up in creative work.

Lead with real value — this week is pro-research-AI, anti-gullibility.

The danger

Where it fails dangerously

  • Invented references — artists, books, movements that don't exist
  • Plausible-wrong history — dates shifted, movements blended
  • Confident citations — links to pages that don't say what's claimed
"All delivered in the same confident voice as the true stuff."

The counter-move

SIFT

Stop — before repeating or reusing a claim
Investigate the source — who actually says this?
Find better coverage — does anyone reputable agree?
Trace to the original — click through to the actual thing

60 seconds per claim.

Mike Caulfield's framework; linked on the week page. Stays on the board all day.

The stakes

Why bother

One invented reference in a crit or client meeting
destroys more credibility than ten good ones build.

Verification is reputation insurance.

This sentence converts the lazy skeptics of verification.

3 minutes

The hallucination trap

"Recommend 4 lesser-known [your field] practitioners from the 20th century and one signature work each."

Image-search ONE. Hands up for ghosts and mismatches.

Lesser-known + signature work = reliable trap. Collect two stories aloud.

Pre-empting the objection

"But it gave me a citation!"

Cited ≠ correct.
Tools cite pages that don't say what's claimed.

Trace to the original. That's the T.

It returns in Q&A — answer it now.

The flattened-references problem

The problem

Everyone gets the same board

"Moodboard ideas for X" → the average of the internet.
Same neon. Same grain. Same Pinterest page, laundered.

Callback to the average machine, weeks 1–2.

The escapes

Three escapes

  1. Ask for territories — named directions with qualities, not images
  2. Demand the non-obvious — "avoid the clichés; flag uncertain references"
  3. Cross domains — geology, deep-sea biology, industrial processes, sport science
"The model has read those corpora too. Almost nobody asks."

Influence vs. copying

First, permission

Everything is a remix

Copy → transform → combine.
How Bowie worked. How Kurosawa worked.
How every movement you love worked.

Influence is how culture functions. The question is the line.

The line

The working test

Influence

Multiple sources, transformed + combined
Qualities extracted
You can narrate the path

Copying

One source, imitated
Signature lifted
You hope nobody sees the reference

"Narratability is the professional test. Can you trace your path out loud in a crit?"
Today's method

The tool

The Influence Triangle

One historical source — verified, ideally public domain
One contemporary source — engaged with, never imitated
One non-art source — science, industry, nature, sport

→ a direction none of them contains alone.

"Three points make a territory. One point makes a tracing." Today's lab.
New discipline

Moodboards

Boards need provenance now

Mark every item:
[real — verified] · [real — unverified] · [AI-generated]

A synthetic "1970s Polish poster" that never existed
will art-direct your whole project from inside your board.

"AI 'references' are outputs wearing research costumes. Label everything."

Lab A · 30 min

Part A

Triangle hunt

  1. Territory expander once → pick the territory that surprises you
  2. Build the triangle: historical + contemporary + non-art
  3. Verify all three exist — SIFT + image search
  4. Source list with [real — verified] marks

Lab B · 25 min

Part B

Mini concept board

6–10 items showing a direction, not a pile.

Real references (source noted) · your sketches/photos welcome
AI items only labelled [AI-generated] — and outnumbered.

All-AI boards get: "Which of these is real? This is currently a hall of mirrors."
