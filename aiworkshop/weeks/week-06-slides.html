Part 1

AI as story interrogator, not story author

~0:10.

The weak move

"Write me a story about a lighthouse keeper."

→ generic three-act sludge. Everyone gets the same one.

Read 3 lines of prepared sludge output, stop mid-sentence, look at the room.

The strong move

Make it ask YOU questions

"Interview me about this character — one question at a time — hunting for what I haven't decided yet."

Your answers are the authorship.

"Its questions surface holes you didn't know you had. Your answers do the designing."

Warning

The yes-and machine

It will enthusiastically extend your worst idea.
It will sand your weirdest idea into screenplay formula.

Keep the weird. The weird is the value.

"The weird detail is the only thing the average can't contain." Returns in critique.

Where story-AI earns it

Genuinely useful

  • Structure audits — "where does my second act sag?"
  • Option generation — "five ways this scene could end"
  • Interview mode — questions that find your holes

Author: you. Always.

Part 1, continued

Character briefs

Design thinking in document form

~0:22.

The brief that works

Five parts

  • Silhouette-first — recognizable as a black shape?
  • 5 traits readable in POSE — not dialogue
  • Costume: 3 signature details — never change
  • The prop they're never without
  • Avoid list — the clichés circling this type
"With AI: the source of truth every generation run is checked against."

The heart of the week

The continuity problem

~0:30.

The evidence

Generate the same character four times

Buttons migrate. Proportions wander. The prop vanishes.

Not a bug being patched soon — a structural weakness.

Show two prepared drifting views side by side NOW — visual proof before the claim.

The plot twist

Animation solved this 90 years ago

Model sheets. Turnarounds. Continuity bibles.

In an AI pipeline these stop being museum pieces.
They're the QA layer.

"Studios hire people who catch and fix drift. Trained eyes. Yours, after today."

Lab · step 1

20 min

Brief by interview

Run the interviewer. Answer every question yourself, specifically.

The brief is assessed on its specificity — its questions, YOUR answers.

Circulate for wholesale-acceptance: "That's its answer. What's yours?"
Lab · steps 2–3

30 min

Sheet, then hunt

Sheet: generate turnarounds — or 3 hand-drawn thumbnails — or the prepared set.

Cop pass: minimum 3 errors annotated. Then trade with a neighbour — find one error in theirs.

The trade is where the skill generalizes. Hand-drawn = "frankly the superior version."

20 min
