The reframe that makes this week work

The deliverable of an image session is not an image — it's a decision trail. Professionals generate wide, kill fast with reasons, and repair by hand. The rejects, and your reasons, are where your taste becomes visible and your authorship becomes defensible.

Opted out of generating?

02Key Concepts

1 · The four legitimate jobs (and the illegitimate one)

JobLooks likeWho uses it
Sketching/exploration20 thumbnails in 10 min to find what you don't wantIllustration, design
Reference & previsLighting studies, mood frames, set ideasMotion, animation, 3D, video
Compositing partsGenerated texture under hand-made foregroundDesign, illustration
Repair & extensionInpaint a region; outpaint a new aspect ratioEveryone in production
The illegitimate job

Final art replacement. Not because a rule says so: it's the part that's actually yours, raw output isn't copyrightable, and clients can buy raw output without you. Generating your final art is competing with yourself at €0/hour.

Why this video: Mike Pound's whiteboard explanation of diffusion — noise to image, steered by text. Knowing the mechanism explains both the magic and the mush.

2 · Image prompt anatomy (your Week 2 brief, visual edition)

  • Subject + action — concrete, specific.
  • Territory qualities — your verified Week 3 vocabulary: materials, light behaviour, colour logic, texture.
  • Composition — framing, viewpoint, and a named empty zone with a purpose ("clear upper third for typography").
  • Medium/process words — "gouache on rough paper" beats "painterly"; "35mm photo" beats "cinematic." Process words invoke physics; style words invoke the average.
  • Avoid list — always includes "no text, no logos, no letters." Models grow fake type; type is yours anyway.

3 · Iteration discipline: wide, then narrow

  1. Generate wide — 4–8 from one brief-shaped prompt.
  2. Kill fast, with reasons — "Kill: texture is mush where the eye lands." One line each.
  3. Select — with a reason too.
  4. Refine narrow — change exactly ONE variable. Rerolling is a slot machine; controlled variation is direction.

4 · Surgical edits: the production skills

  • Inpainting (Generative Fill) — regenerate only a selected region. Your hand chooses what changes.
  • Outpainting (Generative Expand) — extend past the frame; reformat without re-shooting. Verify the invented regions.
  • Image-to-image — your sketch in, rendered options out; strength decides how much of your structure survives. If the composition isn't yours anymore, you gave it too much freedom.
  • Upscaling — invents plausible detail. Inspect at 100% before print.

5 · Rights, tools, and the classroom-safe choice

  • Training tiers: Firefly trains on licensed/Adobe Stock content — the conservative default for classroom and client work. Most other tools train on scraped data; using them is a values decision to make knowingly. (Honest footnote: Firefly's contributor compensation is itself contested — "licensed" is cleaner, not spotless.)
  • Output copyright: purely AI-generated images generally can't be copyrighted; your edits, composition, and combination are what's protectable.
  • Content Credentials (C2PA): tamper-evident "how this was made" metadata. Provenance is becoming infrastructure.
  • Disclosure: generated elements that survive into final work get disclosed, specifically.

04Demo: Campaign Poster — Generate, Reject, Repair

I made this, so can you

A poster background for a contemporary dance festival ("Hinge") taken through the full professional loop.

Prepared demo artifact

Instructor backup / finished example: process-board.html. Use it if the live tool run fails, or to reveal the finished structure after critique.

  1. Brief-shaped prompt: territory qualities + composition with a named type zone + palette + "no people, no text."
  2. Rejection round: 4–6 outputs, keep/kill + one reason each, fast and aloud — and the room overrules one call.
  3. Critique loop: the survivor screenshot into a chat model with the brief — "where will typography fight the image? what reads as AI-generic at arm's length?"

The planned failure

At least one generation ignores "no text" and grows fake letters. It always does. That's why type stays yours.

05Step-by-Step Workflows

From prompt to process board

  1. Write the image brief from your Week 3 territory (or build a quick territory first).
  2. Generate 6–8. Number them. No judging mid-generation.
  3. Kill round: minimum 4 killed, one written reason each — specific beats vibes.
  4. Select + reason. One survivor (two max).
  5. One controlled refinement: change exactly one variable; note what and why.
  6. One surgical edit — inpaint/crop/adjust, by hand where you can.
  7. Board it: iterations in order, decisions visible, final direction + repair notes. (Process documentation template)

Image-to-image from your sketch

  1. Rough sketch (90 seconds, ugly is fine) of your composition.
  2. Photograph/scan → image-to-image with your territory prompt.
  3. Strength high enough that your composition survives.
  4. Treat results as rendering studies of your drawing — steal back what works into the real piece.

06Prompt Recipes

The image brief[Subject + action, concrete]. Visual territory: [3–5 verified qualities — materials, light behaviour, colour logic, texture, process era]. Composition: [framing/viewpoint] with [empty zone + what it's for]. Medium: [process words — e.g., flat screen-print shapes / gouache on rough paper / 35mm photograph]. Palette: [named colours]. Avoid: text, logos, watermarks, [your clichés].
The variation directorKeep everything about option [N] the same, but change only: [one variable — the light direction / the camera height / the palette to X]. Do not change the composition.
The multimodal critiqueCritique this image against my brief: [paste brief]. Where will typography fight the image? What reads as AI-generic at arm's length? What would a print production person flag? Don't soften.
The repair plannerI'm keeping this image but repairing it by hand. Based on my brief, list the 5 highest-impact manual interventions (crop, paint-over, colour, type zone, texture) in priority order, with one sentence on why each earns its place.

The structure (all tracks)

  • Image brief from your territory — 10 min
  • Generate 6–8 — 10 min
  • Kill round with written reasons — 10 min (the core assessment artifact)
  • Select + one controlled refinement — 10 min
  • One surgical edit or crop by hand — 15 min
  • Board assembly + selection notes — 15 min
Zero-generation track

Art-director round: you write the brief and make every keep/kill call; a partner operates. Or run the full kill round + critique + repair plan on the instructor's prepared 8-image set. The decision trail is the deliverable either way.

Free generations ran out?

By design: documentation, critique loop, and repair planning on what you already have completes the deliverable. Nobody is blocked by a quota.

08Integrity Check

This week's four
  1. Could you show this board in a crit and defend every kill?
  2. Is anything on the board secretly "final art" you're hoping to ship raw?
  3. Did you choose your generator knowingly (training data, rights tier) — or by default?
  4. Write the disclosure line now, while the process is fresh.

09Troubleshooting

ProblemFix

It keeps adding text/lettersIt will. "No text, no typography, no letters" — and plan type as your job
Same-y results across promptsYou're rerolling, not directing. Change one named variable — or your territory is too thin; go back to qualities
Hands/faces/details brokenInpaint the region, crop past it, or let it not matter — it's a rough, not a final
Free generations ran outSwitch to kill-round documentation, critique, repair planning — that's most of the deliverable
