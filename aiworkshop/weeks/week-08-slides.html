Part 1

Synthetic users vs. real research

~0:10.

The central metaphor

Personas from a model are

Hypotheses wearing name tags.

Useful as hypotheses. Poison as findings.

Returns in the demo.

Non-negotiable

The three rules

  1. Label everything: HYPOTHESIS — needs real research
  2. Every claim gets a test question
  3. Never present synthetic users as research — that's fabrication
Rule 3, flatly: "It's the kind that gets discovered, and it ends careers."

Live debate

The industry is fighting about this right now

"Synthetic user research" vendors vs. research professionals.

The position that survives scrutiny:
hypotheses → then verification.

NN/g's skeptical video linked on week page. "Be able to argue their side."

Pre-empted

Why bother with personas at all?

Because articulated assumptions beat invisible ones.

The hypothesis forces you to say what you believe — the only way to find out you're wrong cheaply.

Answer to the inevitable question.

Part 2

Edge cases + accessibility

Pessimism as a service

~1:00.

The hunt

The Edge Case Hunt

After any flow:

"8 edge cases, ranked by how badly each damages trust."

Trust-damage ranking = your prioritization tool.

Ranking by trust beats ranking by likelihood — felt severity drives users away.

Accessibility

Specific, then verified

✗ "Ensure good accessibility"
✓ "Photo capture with low vision · colour-only status · touch targets in gloves · screen-reader order"

Then verify against WCAG — the source, not the paraphrase.

"Models paraphrase guidelines loosely. The quick ref has the numbers."

The chain

Plan → prototype

flow → wireframe text → boxes → clickable path

AI accelerates every document before the canvas.
The canvas is still judgment.

Figma AI features churn quarterly — the planning chain works regardless.
