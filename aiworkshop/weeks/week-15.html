The week in one line

Systems outlive tools — leave with a system, not a tool list. Sora's consumer app was retired in April 2026. If your AI skill is "I know how to use [tool]," you're already expiring. If it's a system, you never fall behind — because the system absorbs whatever's new.

02Part 1: Demo Day

Bring the finished(ish) capstone + 1-page process explanation from Week 14. The 3-minute presentation:

  1. The friction (30s) — the real recurring problem, with its weekly cost.
  2. The thing (60s) — show it working; the one decision you're proudest of.
  3. The split (45s) — what AI did, what you did, the honest disclosure.
  4. The verdict (15s) — does it pass the quality bar? What's next?
  5. Questions (30s) — the room probes usefulness and integrity.
What you're being seen for

Not polish — judgment. A small honest tool you can defend beats an ambitious thing you can't explain. The presentation is itself a portfolio rehearsal (Week 13 skills, live).

03Part 2: Future-Proofing (the evaluation system)

New AI products arrive constantly. The skill is evaluating them fast and calmly instead of fear-adopting or fear-refusing. Run any new tool through these six questions in five minutes:

QuestionWhy it matters
What's the friction it actually removes?If you can't name it, you don't need the tool
What's the training-data / rights situation?The Week 4 question — values decision, not default
Control, continuity, cost?The Week 5 video pattern, generalized
What does it touch / where does my data go?The Week 11–12 inspection habit
Does it want my friction or my art?The Week 14 line
What would I disclose if I used it?If you can't write the disclosure, reconsider
The hype filter

Every tool is announced as revolutionary. Wait two weeks; read a skeptic; try it on one real task; check what it actually removed. Most tools are a feature, not a future — the few that matter will still matter in a month.

04Part 3: The Artist's AI Operating System

Your AI OS is a one-pager — honest, not aspirational — with six sections:

  1. Tools I actually pay for — and why. (And what I cancelled — the cancellations are the credibility.)
  2. My folder / naming system — for prompts, process screenshots, iteration archives.
  3. My 5 most-used prompts — the real ones (the critique loop, the brief skeleton, the extraction interview).
  4. My automations — the workflows/tools I trust, and the checkpoint each must pass.
  5. My refusals — what I keep fully human, stated plainly.
  6. My review rhythm — a monthly tool check, one trusted source, what I ignore.

This is the document that makes you durable. Maintain these six sections and you have a working relationship with AI that survives any individual tool dying.

05Part 4: Manifesto v2 (close the loop)

Find your Week 1 Manifesto v1. Read it. You knew less then. Now rewrite it — 8–12 bullets — informed by thirteen weeks of actually doing the work:

  • What boundary held? What boundary moved, and why?
  • What did you learn you'd refuse that you were neutral on in Week 1?
  • What did you learn you'd embrace that you were afraid of?
  • Where does your authorship actually live, now that you've tested it?
The measure of the whole course

The v1→v2 distance is what this course measured. Keep both versions. In a year, write v3 — the habit of revisiting your boundaries is the most durable thing you'll take from here.

07Prompt Recipes

The OS assemblerHelp me turn these rough notes about how I actually use AI into a clean one-page "AI Operating System": [paste your notes on tools, prompts, automations, refusals, rhythm]. Six sections: tools I pay for, folder system, top 5 prompts, automations + checkpoints, refusals, review rhythm. Keep it honest, not aspirational — flag anything that sounds like a system I wish I had rather than one I actually use.
The new-tool evaluatorA new AI tool just launched: [name / what it claims]. Run it through my evaluation pattern: what friction does it actually remove, what's the training-data/rights situation, what about control/continuity/cost, where does my data go, does it want my friction or my art, and what would I disclose if I used it? End with a keep / watch / drop call and your honest reasoning — don't sell it to me.
The Manifesto v2 reflectorHere is my AI Use Manifesto v1 from 13 weeks ago: [paste]. Interview me about what's changed: which boundaries held, which moved and why, what I'd now refuse that I was neutral on, what I'd now embrace that I feared. Don't rewrite it for me — ask the questions that help me write v2 myself.
The hype debunkerThis AI announcement claims to be revolutionary: [paste]. Strip the hype: what can it actually do today (not "will soon"), what are the named limitations, who's the skeptical take, and what's the smallest real task I could test it on this week? Be the calm friend, not the launch livestream.

08Lab: AI OS Checklist + Manifesto v2

Demo Day runs through the middle of the session; the OS/manifesto work bookends it. The deliverables:

  1. AI OS one-pager — the six sections, honest, findable.
  2. Manifesto v2 — v1 rewritten with evidence; keep both versions.
  3. Tool-tracker — the monthly table, started with your current tools.
The AI OS Checklist (your staying-current decision)
  • My monthly 5-minute tool-check date.
  • My ONE trusted source for AI news (not the firehose).
  • What I deliberately ignore.
  • My re-read-the-manifesto cadence.
  • My "try one real task before adopting" rule — confirmed.
Zero-AI track (fully equal)

The OS, manifesto, and tracker are reflective writing — they need no AI at all, and the most honest versions are often written by hand.

09Integrity Check (the last one)

This week's four
  1. Is your AI OS honest — the tools you actually use and refuse, not the impressive version?
  2. Does your Manifesto v2 reflect what you genuinely learned, including where you changed your mind?
  3. Can you still name, plainly, where your authorship lives — and defend it?
  4. Is your system built to keep you distinctive as the feeds fill with slop — or just efficient?

The point of Week 15

This entire course's tool-specific content has a shelf life. The systems, habits, ethics, and judgment do not. That asymmetry is the whole point.

After the course

  • Schedule your first monthly tool-check now — actually put it in your calendar.
  • Share your AI OS one-pager with one classmate; steal one section idea from theirs.
  • In a year: write Manifesto v3. The habit of revisiting your boundaries is the most durable thing you'll take from here.
You're done

You came in possibly skeptical, anxious, or overwhelmed. You leave with a working vocabulary, a briefing skill, an iteration discipline, discipline-specific workflows, a tool or system you built, career materials in your own voice, a capstone — and, most durably, a system and a set of boundaries you can defend. You don't have to love AI. You understand the machine your industry is using, and you've decided, on your own terms, how you'll work with it. Keep your manifesto where you can find it.
