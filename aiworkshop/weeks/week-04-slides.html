Iteration discipline

Wide, then narrow

~0:30.

The loop

The loop

  1. Generate wide — 4–8 from one brief-shaped prompt
  2. Kill fast, with reasons — one line each
  3. Select — also with a reason
  4. Refine narrow — change exactly ONE variable
Board version stays up all day.

The vocabulary

Kill-reasons: the vocabulary of review

✗ "Don't like it"

✓ "Kill — texture is mush where the eye lands"
✓ "Kill — palette drifted warm; brief says bone and rust"
✓ "Keep — the light behaviour is real"

"This is literally how art directors review. Today you build that vocabulary."

The difference

Rerolling vs. directing

Part 1, finale

Surgical edits

The production skills

~0:38.

Surgical 1/4

Inpainting (Generative Fill)

Select a region → regenerate ONLY that.
Fix a prop. Remove an object. Change one element.

Your hand chooses what changes. Surgical, not slot machine.

The skill that transfers to real client work immediately.

Surgical 2/4

Outpainting (Generative Expand)

Extend past the frame.
A4 poster → wide banner without re-shooting.

Verify the invented regions.

Campaign reformatting is a real junior-designer task; this halves it.

Surgical 3/4

Image-to-image: your sketch, its rendering

Your rough drawing in → rendered options out.
Strength = how much of YOUR structure survives.

If the composition isn't yours anymore, you gave it too much freedom.

"The bridge between rolling dice and directing." For students mourning their drawing time — this is their lane.

Surgical 4/4

Upscaling invents detail

Plausible texture, hallucinated exactly where it matters.

Inspect at 100% before print. Always.

Quick war story if you have one (type edges, skin, fabric weave).

Ethics

Rights 1/4

Training data tiers

Firefly: licensed + Adobe Stock → the classroom-safe default
Most others: scraped data → a values decision

Make it knowingly, not by default.

Honest footnote for Q&A: Firefly contributor compensation is itself contested. "Licensed" is cleaner, not spotless.

Rights 2/4

Output copyright

Purely AI-generated images: generally not copyrightable.
Your edits, composition, combination: protectable.

The law keeps siding with your craft.

US Copyright Office guidance linked on week page; EU broadly similar on human authorship.

Stage 1

The brief-shaped prompt

Poster background for a contemporary dance festival called "Hinge." Visual territory: industrial materials under stage light — folded steel, tensioned fabric, raking warm light, deep shadow. Bold abstract composition with clear empty zone in the upper third for typography. Muted palette: rust, bone, near-black. No people, no text, no logos.
Narrate the anatomy while it generates: "named empty zone — type needs somewhere to live." Number outputs 1–6.
Demo

Stage 2

The rejection round

Six outputs. Keep/kill + one reason each. Fast. Aloud.

Then: overrule me.

Take one overrule seriously and reverse the call with their reason on record. The room doing kill-reasons IS the lesson.
Demo

Stage 3

Critique the survivor

Critique this poster background against my brief [paste brief]. Where will typography fight the image? What reads as AI-generic at arm's length? What would a print production person flag?
Screenshot into chat tool. Read the two sharpest points; reject one critique point with a reason — filtering critique is also modelled.
Demo

Stage 4

Repair by hand

Crop for the type zone · Generative Fill ONE region · levels · place real type.

Five minutes of recognizable design craft. The part that's yours.

Slow down here. This is when skeptical students decide the week is legitimate.

Lab

Your subject

By track

  • Illustration: book cover rough for a folk tale you like
  • Graphic design: gig poster background with a named type zone
  • Animation/motion: one shot, three light moods (style frames)
  • 3D: previs of a set you might actually build
Same structure for all; subject is the only variable.
The graded part

Kill round

Minimum 4 kills, one written reason each

✓ "Palette drifted warm; brief says bone/rust"
✗ "Don't like it"

Circulate upgrading vague reasons live: "What drifted — palette, texture, composition?"
Always available

Alternative

Zero-generation track

Art-director round: you brief + judge; a partner operates.
Or: full kill round + critique + repair plan on the prepared 8-image set.

The decision trail is the deliverable either way.

Check in personally; these boards are often the sharpest in the room.
