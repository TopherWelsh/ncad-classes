01This Week at a Glance

You will make: a 15-second motion treatment pack — one of three directions, fully developed.

1 · Concepts (40 min)

The pipeline map; the treatment's five slots; shot-list audit habits; the style-frame bridge.

2 · Reality check (15 min)

Video generation, honestly: control, continuity, cost, rights.

3 · Demo (35 min)

Tide pools: treatment → shot list → AE task list → style frame prompt.

4 · Lab + pitch (110 min)

One Prompt, Three Treatments → your full pack → 90-second pitches.

Why this matters in real work

Motion design lives or dies in preproduction — and preproduction is exactly where AI is genuinely strong, because treatments, shot lists, and task lists are structured, checkable documents. Video generation is the least-ready part: studios use it for mood and previs, not finals. AI is your production assistant, not your animator.

02Key Concepts

1 · Where AI sits in the motion pipeline

StageAI usefulnessWhy
Brief / treatment★★★Options + structure; your taste picks
Script / VO draft★★★Drafts fast; your voice edits
Storyboard / shot list★★★Structured + checkable; timing maths needs you
Style frames★★Week 4 skills; previs-quality, not finals
Animation itself★Keyframes, easing, acting — the job stays yours
Video generation★ (previs/mood)Continuity + control weak; credits cost money
Render / export / admin★★★Checklists, naming, specs — pure friction

2 · The treatment: 120 words that decide everything

Five slots, every time: visual approach (territory language), pacing logic (how time behaves), type behaviour (type is a character — say how it acts), palette (named, limited), sound feeling (one line changes everything).

The edit-two-lines move

AI drafts the treatment; you rewrite the two lines that matter. After that it's yours — and you'll feel the difference.

3 · Shot lists: structured, checkable, slightly wrong

Shot #, duration, action, camera, type on screen, asset needed, transition out. AI builds these fast, with two reliable failures:

Watch out — the two failures
  • Durations don't sum. You asked for 15 seconds; it produced 17 in a confident table. It does arithmetic-shaped output — you do arithmetic.
  • Fantasy shots. It will specify the fluid sim you can't make by Friday. Killing those shots isn't failure — it's called producing.

4 · Style frames: the Week 4 bridge

Brief-shaped prompt, type zone reserved, kill round, repair. Generate the world; comp the type yourself — models still mangle letterforms, and type in motion is your craft.

5 · Video generation: the honest state (mid-2026)

  • Good for: mood reels, pitch previs, abstract texture, image-to-video on your own style frames.
  • Bad at: continuity across shots, precise art direction, type, anything approved frame-by-frame.
  • Costly: credits burn fast; queues eat lab time.
  • The evaluation pattern that outlives every tool: control · continuity · cost · rights. (Gen-4, Veo, Kling today; Sora's consumer app already died once.)

04Demo: Tide Pools — Concept to Production Plan

I made this, so can you

A rough concept ("20-second title sequence for a documentary about tide pools") through four moves — every artifact checkable, every one edited by a human.

Prepared demo artifact

Instructor backup / finished example: shotlist.html. Use it if the live tool run fails, or to reveal the finished structure after critique.

  1. Treatment (120 words, five slots) — then two lines edited live. Taste, visibly applied.
  2. Shot list — 7–9 shots, durations must total exactly 20s, hardest shots flagged. The sum is wrong on the first try; watch the recount.
  3. AE task list — grouped by pre-comp, assets, animation, finishing — then the producer's edit: merge, cut, halve the fantasy hours.
  4. Style frame prompt for shot 3, type zone reserved — Week 4 skills aimed at motion.
What to notice

Every artifact is checkable; the human edit pass tightened all four; the chain reuses for any motion job — kinetic type, product spot, title sequence, gig-screen loop.

05Step-by-Step Workflows

The preproduction chain (any motion job)

  1. Brief (Week 2 skeleton): message, audience, duration, platform, constraints.
  2. Treatment ×3: three contrasting directions; pick one; rewrite its two most load-bearing lines yourself.
  3. Shot list: durations sum to spec (verify by hand), assets named, hardest shots flagged.
  4. Audit pass: kill fantasy shots; re-spec for your real toolkit and deadline.
  5. Style frames: 1–3 key moments, Week 4 discipline, type zones reserved.
  6. Task list: grouped, estimated — then re-estimated by you (halve its optimism).
  7. Document: the pack is your process evidence and your pitch artifact.

The 60-second timing audit

  • Sum the durations column — by hand, not by trusting.
  • First 2 seconds: does something earn attention?
  • Breath: one hold ≥ 1.5s, or is it all churn?
  • The out: does the last shot resolve, or just stop?

06Prompt Recipes

The treatment writerAct as a motion design director. Concept: [piece, duration, platform, audience]. Write a treatment in 120 words covering exactly: visual approach (use this territory: [your verified qualities]), pacing logic, type behaviour, palette, sound feeling. No shot list yet. Then list the 2 biggest risks in this direction.
The three-treatment expander (lab core)Take this one message: "[your message]". Give me THREE contrasting 15-second treatments: (1) kinetic typography — type is the only performer; (2) product/object spot — one hero object, real-world light; (3) abstract title sequence — texture and motion carry the feeling, type minimal. Each: 100 words, the five treatment slots, one risk. Don't pick a winner — that's my job.
The shot list builderBreak my chosen treatment into a shot list table: shot #, duration (must total EXACTLY [15] seconds), action, camera/movement, type on screen, asset needed (2D anim / footage / generated still / pure type), transition out. 5–8 shots. Flag the two hardest shots and say why. Then list every asset I must gather or make, as a checklist.
The AE task list generatorFrom this shot list, build my After Effects task checklist grouped by: project/pre-comp setup, assets to gather or make, animation tasks per shot, finishing (grade, sound placement, exports for [platforms]). Estimate hours per group honestly, then add 30% because you're an optimist.
The timing doctorHere is my shot list: [paste]. Audit it like a broadcast producer: do the durations sum to [15s]? Does the first 2 seconds earn attention? Where does the piece breathe? Does the ending resolve or just stop? Suggest the single highest-impact timing change.
The style frame promptWrite one image-generation prompt for the style frame of shot [N], strictly in this treatment's territory: [paste treatment]. Composition must reserve [zone] for typography. Medium/process words, palette named, no text in the image.

07Lab: One Prompt, Three Treatments (70 min)

Cold start friendly. No message of your own? Standing one: "Slow down — Dublin moves better when it breathes."

  1. Three treatments (15 min): run the expander; pick one + write two sentences on why — the pick rationale is assessed thinking.
  2. Edit the treatment (10 min): rewrite its two most load-bearing lines in your own words.
  3. Shot list (15 min): build it; run the timing audit by hand; kill/re-spec fantasy shots.
  4. Style frame (15 min): generate one frame (Week 4 discipline) or write the full frame brief + thumbnail sketch.
  5. Task list (10 min): generate, then producer's edit — cross out, merge, re-estimate.
  6. Pack it (5 min): treatment + shot list + frame + task list.
Zero-generation track

The entire pack except the style frame image is text. The frame becomes prompt + sketch — which is exactly what studios call a frame brief. Equal credit, professional document.

Pairs option

One "director" + one "producer" per pack works beautifully — swap roles at the shot list.

08Integrity Check

This week's four
  1. Which two treatment lines did you rewrite — are they actually yours now?
  2. Did you verify the timing maths yourself?
  3. Client pack: planning documents usually need only a process note; generated style frames need disclosure.
  4. Generated scratch music? Check the rights state first — it's contested. Never client deliverables.

09Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
Treatments all feel like adsYour message is a slogan. Give it a feeling + audience; ban advertising clichés explicitly
Durations don't sumThey won't. Recount yourself; tell it the correct total and which shots to trim
Shot list demands skills you don't haveGood catch — that's producing. "Replace shots needing [3D/sim] with achievable 2D alternatives"
Style frame ignores the type zoneName the zone AND its purpose; regenerate or crop to create it
Hour estimates are fantasyAlways. "+30% because you're an optimist" helps; your own history helps more
Type animation ideas feel genericModels suggest the same 6 type behaviours. Use them as the banned list; design the seventh
