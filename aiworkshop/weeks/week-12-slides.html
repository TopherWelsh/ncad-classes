Part 1

Agent vs chatbot vs workflow

~0:10.

Three things, not one

Sort them

  • Chatbot — answers; you drive each turn; low risk
  • Workflow — fixed steps you defined; you wrote the path
  • Agent — picks its own steps; higher risk
Most students conflate all three. This sorts them.

The reframe

You don't need a robot.

Most "agentic" creative work is a workflow — predictable, repeatable, safe — with one or two agentic steps.

You need an assembly line with a human inspector.

Anthropic's own engineers: prefer the simplest pattern that works.

Part 1, continued

Tool use

Where capability and danger both live

~0:22.

Every tool is a risk surface

Smallest access that does the job

An agent's power = tool use: read/write files, run code, search, call services.

"A folder-organizer needs to move files. It does not need the internet or the power to delete."

Why scope tightly

Broad access is how

"organize my folder"

becomes "email my drafts to a client."

Concrete, memorable, true. Scope is a safety decision.

Part 1, the thesis

The checkpoint

Where you stay the director

~0:30.

The pattern

Plan first, act second

Make the agent propose its plan —
and STOP for approval before touching anything.

Two words: plan first. The whole safety pattern compresses to this.

Non-negotiable

A checkpoint before every irreversible action.

Sending · deleting · publishing · paying · overwriting.

"Irreversible + outward-facing = highest checkpoint category."

The whole difference

Twenty seconds

Reading a plan and killing one bad line is the entire difference between

delegating and abdicating.

You'll demo exactly this. Reference it forward.

Part 1, the seatbelt

Safety rails

~0:38. Absolute, like Blender week.

Design these in, every time

The four rails

  1. Never delete — move to /_review
  2. Run on copies — your only copy is sacred
  3. Changelog everything — every action + every uncertainty
  4. Flag uncertainty — skip and report, don't guess
"Not best practices. The seatbelt."

Watch out

It will invent a decision you never gave it

A naming rule, an alt-text guess, a "this is probably the final."

The changelog and /_review catch those. So read the log.

Rails don't prevent improvisation; they make it recoverable and visible.

Part 2

The Automation Boundary Map

~1:00.

Sort everything three ways

Three columns

Automate — friction, repeatable, reversible, taste-free
Assist — AI helps, a human decides every time
Human-only — your authorship, your relationships

All three columns should be full.

The warning sign

An empty "human-only" column

is a warning sign.

If nothing is sacred, something is about to be automated that shouldn't be.

Callback to Week 1's manifesto — this is its operational form.

Candidates

Good agent tasks

Structured · repetitive · reversible · verifiable.

Batch renaming · alt-text drafts · folder packaging · contact sheets · QA checklists · admin scaffolding

"Not all four? It wants assist, not automate."
