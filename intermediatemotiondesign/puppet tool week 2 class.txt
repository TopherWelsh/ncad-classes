    <h2 style="color: #888; font-size: 14px; text-transform: uppercase; border-bottom: 1px solid #ccc; padding-bottom: 5px; margin-top: 40px;">2. Mastering the Mesh & Motion</h2>
    <div style="display: flex; flex-wrap: wrap; gap: 20px; margin-top: 20px; margin-bottom: 40px;">
        <!-- Mesh Workflow -->
        <div style="flex: 1; min-width: 450px; background: #f1f3f5; padding: 25px; border-radius: 12px;">
            <h3 style="margin-top: 0;">📐 The Mesh Anatomy</h3>
            <p>When you place your first pin, After Effects generates a <strong>triangulated mesh</strong> over your alpha pixels. To keep your animation smooth, you must adjust the toolbar settings:</p>
            <ul>
                <li><strong>Expansion:</strong> The "buffer" around your artwork. Increase this if your edges are being cut off by the warp.</li>
                <li><strong>Density:</strong> Adds more triangles. Higher density = smoother bends but slower render times.</li>
                <li><strong>Pre-Compose First:</strong> Always pre-compose your character artwork (Move All Attributes) before pinning to keep your mesh clean.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
        <!-- Recording Workflow -->
        <div style="flex: 1; min-width: 400px; background: #1a1a1a; color: white; padding: 25px; border-radius: 12px;">
            <h3 style="margin-top: 0; color: #00c6ff;">🎙️ Live Motion Recording</h3>
            <p>You don't have to manually set every keyframe. You can "perform" your character:</p>
            <ol>
                <li>Select a Position Pin.</li>
                <li>Hold <kbd style="background: #333; padding: 2px 5px; border-radius: 4px; border: 1px solid #555;">Ctrl</kbd> (PC) or <kbd style="background: #333; padding: 2px 5px; border-radius: 4px; border: 1px solid #555;">Cmd</kbd> (Mac).</li>
                <li><strong>Click and Drag</strong> the pin. AE will record your mouse movement in real-time as keyframes.</li>
            </ol>
            <p style="font-size: 0.9em; color: #aaa;"><em>Nugget: Use 'Motion Sketch' recording options to increase smoothing and eliminate "jittery" mouse hand.</em></p>
        </div>
    </div>

    <!-- ⚠️ PITFALL WARNING: THE MESH PULL -->
    <div style="background-color: #ffebee; padding: 25px 25px 25px 60px; border: 2px solid #c62828; border-radius: 12px; margin-bottom: 40px; position: relative;">
        <span style="position: absolute; top: 25px; left: 20px; font-size: 1.8em;">⚠️</span>
        <strong style="color: #b71c1c; text-transform: uppercase; font-size: 0.9em; display: block; margin-bottom: 5px;">Common Pitfall: The Artwork "Pull"</strong>
        <p style="margin: 0; color: #4a0404;">If your character's arm is touching its torso in the original artwork, the Puppet Tool treats them as <strong>one single piece of rubber</strong>. When you move the arm, the torso will "pull" and stretch with it. To fix this, you must separate your artwork into different layers in Illustrator/Photoshop before bringing it into AE. <strong>If they aren't separated, the mesh can't be independent.</strong></p>
    </div>

    <!-- 💡 PRO-TIP: LOOPING -->

        <strong style="color: #f57f17; text-transform: uppercase; font-size: 0.9em; display: block; margin-bottom: 5px;">Pro-Tip: Endless Secondary Action</strong>
        <p style="margin: 0; color: #3e2723;">Once you've animated a subtle wave or sway, Alt-Click the pin's position stopwatch and type <code>loopOut("pingpong")</code>. This will keep that subtle "life" moving forever, allowing you to focus on the character's primary movements.</p>
