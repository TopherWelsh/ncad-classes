            <h3 style="margin-top: 0; color: #212529;">🖼️ Part 1: The Moodboard</h3>
            <p>Your moodboard should explicitly define your <strong>Visual Anchor</strong>. This includes:</p>
            <ul style="padding-left: 20px;">
                <li><strong>Hero Identity:</strong> The final design of your shape or character.</li>
                <li><strong>Color Palette:</strong> HEX codes that convey the "teaching" tone.</li>
                <li><strong>Typography:</strong> UI fonts for labels and principles.</li>
                <li><strong>Texture/Style:</strong> 2D, 3D, Grainy, Glassmorphism, etc.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
        <!-- Column 2: Storyboard -->
        <div style="flex: 1; min-width: 300px; padding: 20px; background: #f1f3f5; border-radius: 10px; border-top: 4px solid #0076c0;">
            <h3 style="margin-top: 0; color: #212529;">📑 Part 2: The Storyboard</h3>
            <p>Map the 90-second journey. You must show how one scene flows into the next.</p>
            <ul style="padding-left: 20px;">
                <li><strong>Scene Breaks:</strong> One frame per principle (minimum).</li>
                <li><strong>Transitions:</strong> How does "Anticipation" physically become "Arcs"?</li>
                <li><strong>Labels:</strong> Where will the text live on screen?</li>
                <li><strong>Narrative:</strong> Include script notes under each frame.</li>
            </ul>
        </div>
    </div>

            <p style="margin: 0; color: #4a148c;">Appeal is not "polished later." In this phase, ask yourself: <em>Does this character have charisma? Is the color palette magnetic?</em> If the design is boring in the storyboard, it will be boring in the final render. <strong>Appeal starts now.</strong></p>
