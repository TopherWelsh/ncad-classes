Today's Filter

Clarity is not the absence of style. It is style with a job. Every graphic, cut, movement, transition, and sound cue must earn its place.

Before You Animate More

Production Gate

  • The concept promise is specific.
  • The audience and viewing format are defined.
  • The factual source or claim is verified.
  • The relationship has a verb: compare, grow, flow, rank, connect, transform, or cause.
  • The motif and visual grammar are consistent.
  • The storyboard has a readable progression and payoff.
  • The hardest technical or storytelling move is proven.
  • The visuals communicate more than the voiceover or text alone.

Today's Four-Hour Production Sprint

  • 8:00–8:25: Stand-up and approval gate—claim, audience, format, source, motif, storyboard, and current blocker.
  • 8:25–9:20: Build the hardest proof first: the transition, data behavior, character action, compositing shot, or system that could break the piece.
  • 9:20–10:15: Targeted mini-demo and production block for charts, counters, icon systems, transitions, reusable comps, or timing.
  • 10:15–10:40: First-view clarity testing. Show the work cold and do not explain it before playback.
  • 10:40–11:35: Revision pass—fix comprehension first, then eye direction, pacing, continuity, consistency, sound, and finish.
  • 11:35–12:00: Export, watch the actual file from beginning to end, and preflight the final package.

The 60-Second Clarity Test

👁️ Show It Cold

The viewer should understand the work they actually experience—not the explanation you gave before pressing play.

1. Play once

Do not introduce the topic or explain the intended takeaway.

2. Ask what landed

“What did you understand?” Record the answer before defending your choices.

3. Find the break

“Where did you get lost, stop reading, or misunderstand the point?”

4. Check the system

Ask what visual rule or motif they noticed and what it seemed to mean.

💡 Revision Priority

Misunderstanding → missing information → eye direction → pacing → polish. Do not spend an hour polishing a shot that still communicates the wrong idea.

Animated Infographic Rubric • 100 Points

Story, clarity + information design • 25

  • Focused concept with hook, progression, and payoff.
  • Information is sequenced into digestible beats.
  • Graphics add explanation rather than repeat words.
  • Facts are represented honestly and credited.

Motif, symbolism + visual system • 20

  • A deliberate motif connects the piece.
  • Symbolism and visual metaphor support the subject.
  • Type, color, imagery, and composition are cohesive.
  • Mixed techniques remain in one visual world.

Motion, pacing + storytelling • 20

  • Motion principles are intentional.
  • Pacing is neither rushed nor slow.
  • Holds and transitions support comprehension.
  • Movement is tailored to content rather than generic.

Framing, eye direction + format • 15

  • Each shot has a clear focal point.
  • Scale, contrast, position, direction, and timing lead the eye.
  • Camera choices serve the story.
  • Text stays readable in the chosen format.

Craft, polish + sound • 15

  • Animation, compositing, typography, and transitions are refined.
  • No accidental pops, clipping, broken links, or unfinished frames.
  • Audio is clean and intentional.
  • The result is credible as a demo-reel piece.

Process, presentation + delivery • 5

  • Required planning and development work is present.
  • Sources and assets are credited.
  • Files are named and packaged correctly.
  • You can explain what the motion contributes.

Final Technical QA

  • Duration is 15–30 seconds.
  • Text is readable at the intended size.
  • No elements clip outside safe areas.
  • Audio edits and levels are clean.
  • Spelling, credits, and filenames are correct.
  • The final export was watched beginning to end.
  • Placeholder artwork and temporary audio are removed.
  • The project is packaged with linked assets.
  • The first three seconds earn attention.
  • The result is ready to show an employer or client.
